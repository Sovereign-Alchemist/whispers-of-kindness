Torn edge, side by side

Open this from disk and compare the three. The middle panel is the polygon that was in the page. The right panel is what replaced it. Both are the same rectangle, the same cream, and the same angle, so the edge is the only thing that differs.

1. The reference

Pela's photograph. No two teeth alike, some deep, some barely there, and the sheet does not lie flat.

2. The old polygon

Every tooth the same depth and the same spacing, because each one was typed. This is the thing that read as decorative trim.

3. Turbulence, scale 22

Fractal noise displacing the edge. No period, so no repeat. The shadow is a drop-shadow after the displacement, so it follows the ragged silhouette rather than the original rectangle.

4. The photograph, cut out

The transparent version, at the same size and angle as the generated sheets. This is the honest comparison: whether the generated edge is close enough, or whether the real one should mask the top layer.

What to look for, in this order:

  • Teeth that vary. Some should bite several pixels in, some should barely dent the edge.
  • No rhythm. If you can find a repeating beat along the top, the frequency is still too high.
  • Corners that are not sharp. A torn sheet has no square corners left.
  • A shadow that follows the tearing rather than sitting behind a straight line.

If it is too ragged or too tame, the number to change is scale. There are three of them in index.html, one per paper layer: cream 22, kraft 20, sage 24. They are kept in that relationship on purpose, the top sheet tearing hardest, so raising one means raising all three by the same ratio. Higher tears deeper. baseFrequency controls how often the edge wanders, and lower is slower and longer.

Raising scale means widening the filter region too. Displacement pushes paper outside the element's box, and the region is what decides how far outside it may go. Too small a region cuts the deepest teeth off in a straight line, which puts back the exact fault the whole change was made to remove, and it does it only at the extremes where it is hardest to notice. The three filters are at 124% for this reason, and the 34 sample in this file is at 136%.

The photograph is now a real option, since the cut-out version has a genuine alpha channel. Verified rather than assumed: corners read A=0, the centre reads A=255 on cream, and 37% of the canvas is transparent. If panel 4 beats panel 3 to your eye, the move is to mask the top cream layer with it and leave the kraft and sage generated, so the one photographed edge never repeats itself three times.
